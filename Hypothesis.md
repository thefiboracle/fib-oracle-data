# PRE-REGISTERED HYPOTHESIS

Committed 5 August 2026, at n=3.

## Claim under test

Hart et al. (2013, Frontiers in Zoology) found dogs preferentially align on the
north-south magnetic axis when defecating, and that this alignment breaks down
during periods of geomagnetic disturbance.

## Definitions

- **Aligned**: bearing within 350-010 degrees or 170-190 degrees (+/-20 degrees of the N-S axis)
- **Quiet field**: Kp < 3
- **Disturbed field**: Kp >= 3

## Prediction

The aligned window covers 80 of 360 degrees, so chance alignment is 22.2%.

If the effect is present in Subject 001, alignment across quiet-field readings
should exceed 22.2% by a material margin.

## Reporting point

n = 100.

## What would refute this

Alignment at or below approximately 22% on quiet-field readings at n=100 indicates
the effect is absent in this subject under these conditions.

## Known confounds

- Subject is walked on-lead along streets. Street orientation biases bearing.
- Single subject. No control animal.
- Bearings read by hand from a phone magnetometer. Accuracy +/-5 degrees at best.
- Observer is not blind to the hypothesis.

## Commitment

These definitions will not be changed after seeing more data.
Any change will be committed separately, dated, and announced in advance.
## AMENDMENT 1 — 8 August 2026

Correction. The original document described the alignment window as
"+/-20 degrees" and calculated chance alignment at 22.2% from an 80-degree window.

Both were wrong. The stated numeric windows — 350-010 and 170-190 — total
40 degrees, which is +/-10 degrees either side of the axis. Chance alignment
is therefore 11.1%.

The numeric windows are unchanged and have been applied correctly to every
reading to date. No ruling changes. Only the description and the chance
figure are corrected.

This makes the test harder to pass, not easier.
